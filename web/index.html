The community decides who uses its mental-health data, for what, and what comes back.

Consent Commons is an open-source prototype of governance infrastructure for a mental-health data commons: portable consent terms, a plain-language consent chat in Pidgin, Yorùbá and English, a community review panel, and a tamper-evident ledger anyone can audit.

Why this exists

Mental-health data from African communities is increasingly used to build AI, often without meaningful consent, community control, or any share of the benefits. This prototype explores the opposite: the people the data comes from set the terms, and the terms are enforced by code, not just policy.

Synthetic demo. Every person, organisation, reviewer and record here is made up. There is no real health data in this project, and nothing you type leaves your browser.

How it works

1 · UnderstandA chat explains each choice in plain words. Four quick questions check it is clear before anything is recorded.
2 · ConsentChoices become machine-readable terms. The person keeps a signed receipt and a private withdrawal code.
3 · RequestA researcher asks for data. Automatic checks drop everyone whose terms don't cover it, and block the request if too few remain.
4 · ReviewA panel of youth, lived-experience and health-worker reps votes. Each vote is bound to the exact request. Community seats can block.
5 · ReleaseData goes out with a signed manifest: the binding terms, benefit obligations, and which consent each record came from.
6 · WithdrawOne message stops all future use, and recipients are told to delete the person's record. Works from the chat or a basic phone over USSD (simulated here).
CONSENT FLOW

Consent chat

WhatsApp-style. English, Nigerian Pidgin and Yorùbá (draft translations, flagged for native-speaker review). Comprehension check, receipt, check and withdraw.
ACCESS REVIEW

Community panel

Researchers submit requests and see a live preview of what consent allows. 5 seats, quorum with a community majority, reasoned votes, digest-bound approvals, benefit tracking.
LEDGER

Tamper-evident record

Every event is hash-chained. Verify it, replay every governance rule, and try the tamper lab: edit, delete, or rewrite history and watch what catches it.
LOW CONNECTIVITY

USSD on any phone

A clickable simulation of *347*22# (example code): check your consent or take your yes back with a basic phone and no data. Same ledger, same rules as the chat. The chat also has an "assisted by a health worker" label.
COMMUNITY MEETINGS

Plain-words release summary

Who got the data, for what, the vote, the rules and what is owed back, in English, Pidgin and Yorùbá, with a check against the ledger. Printable.
CONSENT SCHEMA

Terms that travel

Versioned JSON Schemas for consent terms, access requests, release manifests, receipts and ledger entries. /schema/consent-terms.v1.json

What this is, and is not

  • An independent prototype built by Joshua Jubelo in response to a published brief from the Slum & Rural Health Initiative (SRHIN) in the Mozilla Foundation 2027 Fellowship call. It is not an SRHIN product, and it is not endorsed by SRHIN or Mozilla.
  • Not a chatbot and not a clinical tool. It is the governance layer that would sit around tools that already exist.
  • Not yet tested with real people. Translations are unreviewed drafts. It must be co-designed with young people, people with lived experience and community health workers before any real use.
  • Not legal advice and not a compliance claim (for example under Nigeria's Data Protection Act 2023).

Open by default

Apache-2.0 licensed. No accounts, no tracking, no paid services: the whole demo runs in your browser on Web Crypto (SHA-256 and Ed25519). Anyone can audit a ledger with the command-line tool.

npx github:Quantumwoof/consent-commons verify ledger.jsonl
